Debt and the Energy Transition: A Ticking Clock for Emerging Economies

Published April 4, 2025 • 9 min read

Emerging economies face a double challenge: decarbonize rapidly while managing fragile public finances. Mounting debt burdens — exacerbated by IMF loan conditions and global interest rate hikes — limit fiscal space for long-term green investment.

This post explores how climate finance tools, green bonds, and debt-for-climate swaps can help create fiscal breathing room. But without structural reforms in how the global financial architecture treats development and climate as inseparable, the transition will stall.

Tunisia’s case illustrates these tensions: while it has ambitious solar goals, its fiscal rigidity and IMF constraints often push urgent debt obligations ahead of transformative investments.
