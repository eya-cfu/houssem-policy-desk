Rethinking Carbon Pricing in Resource-Constrained Economies

Published: April 4, 2025

In many emerging economies, carbon pricing is often framed as a luxury—something to consider only after basic development goals are met. But this dichotomy is misleading. When designed thoughtfully, carbon pricing can serve both fiscal and environmental purposes.

This post explores how a context-specific approach—integrating subsidies reform, social compensation mechanisms, and progressive tax structures—can make carbon pricing politically and economically feasible even in low-income countries.

We also discuss how international finance mechanisms and green transition support can de-risk early adoption and encourage reform coalitions across civil society, government, and industry.
